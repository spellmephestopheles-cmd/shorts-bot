1. [TOPICAL] The Robot That Can Beat Any Chess Master Can't Fold Your Laundry
Paradox: A computer beat the world chess champion in 1997. In 2026, robots still struggle with socks.
Tension: Chess has about 10^120 possible games. A towel has no rules. A sock changes shape every time you touch it.
Pivot: You see, "easy" for humans took evolution a billion years to master. Chess took us a few centuries (Moravec's paradox).
Twist: The skills you think are simple are the most complex thing your brain does, so a humanoid cleaning your flat is the real miracle.
Virality: 4.4/5 (strongest: hook + relatable + timely with humanoid-robot buzz)

2. [INDIA/ASIA] The Country Where a Tea Seller Beats Banks at Payments
Paradox: A man selling chai on a pavement runs a payment network more advanced than most banks.
Tension: Just a paper QR sticker. No card machine. No fee. Settled in seconds.
Pivot: You see, India built UPI as a public, open system, so any app could plug in, and it reportedly handles around half of the world's real-time payments.
Twist: Countries with the richest banks are now copying a system designed for the poorest street vendor.
Virality: 4.3/5 (strongest: shareability + surprising global flip)

3. Why Your Headphones Are Named After a Dead Viking King
Paradox: Every phone on Earth carries the name of a Viking who died 1,000+ years ago.
Tension: Bluetooth. Not a tech word. Not an acronym. The logo is a secret rune code.
Pivot: You see, King Harald "Bluetooth" Gormsson united warring Danish tribes. Engineers wanted a name for a tech that united rival devices.
Twist: The logo merges two of his initials in runes, and the name was only meant as a placeholder.
Virality: 4.1/5 (strongest: curiosity gap + rewatch twist)

4. [TOPICAL] The Moon Is Leaving, and Your Day Is Getting Longer
Paradox: Every year, the Moon steals a bit of Earth's time.
Tension: It drifts away about 3.8 cm a year. Days were about 19 hours long 1.4 billion years ago. No one felt it happen.
Pivot: You see, tides act like brakes on Earth's spin, and that energy pushes the Moon outward.
Twist: Eventually a day could be much longer, and total solar eclipses will end because the Moon will look too small.
Virality: 4.2/5 (strongest: scale + scroll-stopping first line)

5. [INDIA/ASIA] The Zero That Was Hiding in an Indian Manuscript
Paradox: The most important number in history started as a dot.
Tension: Without it, no computers, no phones, no money system. Greeks and Romans never had it.
Pivot: You see, in the Bakhshali manuscript, found in a village in modern Pakistan, dots stand for placeholders, carbon dated to as early as the 3rd-4th century.
Twist: The idea of zero as a number came from Indian mathematicians, and every screen you use runs on 0 and 1.
Virality: 4.0/5 (strongest: universal hook + twist about modern life)
